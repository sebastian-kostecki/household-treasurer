# Category Group: one order per type, and the group row in the Budget

ADR-0063 defines a Category Group (a named set of Categories of one type, no plan, no settings, unique name within the type, deleting it leaves its Categories without a group) but leaves open where a group stands in the list, what dragging does, and what the Budget shows for it. S-22 (FR-004, FR-019, FR-021, FR-022) needs these answered before the code. This ADR adds to ADR-0063 and changes none of its clauses.

Type. The type of a group is fixed when it is created. A group accepts only Categories of its type. A mistake is fixed by deleting the group and creating a new one. A type change of a Category takes it off its group (ADR-0063) and puts it at the end of the order of its new type.

One order per type. The order of the list is one sequence of `sort_order` per User and type, shared by groups and by Categories without a group, with the Categories of a group standing right after it. Example: Kredyt 0, Opłaty stałe 1, Czynsz 2, Prąd 3, Wakacje 4, Zdrowie 5, Apteka 6, where „Opłaty stałe” and „Zdrowie” are groups. So every reader that sorts Budget Lines by `categories.sort_order` (`BudgetLine::scopeOrdered`) keeps working and gets the Categories of a group next to each other. The sequence is always renumbered 0 to n-1 on a write. A tree is built from (group membership, `sort_order`), so it is unambiguous even when the sequence is inconsistent (gaps and ties exist before the first write: a type change does not renumber and the demo seeder numbers both types together). Ties are broken by `sort_order`, then a group before a Category, then `name_key`, then id; the same rule is used by the PHP class that writes the order and by the client that builds the tree. A new group, a new Category and a Category after a type change go to the end of the sequence of the type, counted over Categories and groups. Assigning a Category to a group puts it at the end of that group; „Bez grupy” puts it at the end of the sequence.

Dragging. Dragging in „Kategorie” and in the Budget changes both the order and the group of a Category, and groups can be moved among Categories without a group. Both are attributes of the list Category, so they apply to all years (ADR-0063); a 2025 view does not keep the old groups. The Budget sees only the active lines of one year and the groups that have at least one of them; „Kategorie” see the active Categories and all groups of the type. A write places the visible items, in the new order, into the places they held in the current sequence; hidden Categories keep their group and their place, and a hidden Category of a moved group moves with it. Deleting a group leaves its Categories in the sequence where they were, without a group; only the place of the group disappears.

The group row in the Budget. A group has a row with the same columns as „Razem”. Its sums (Initial Plan, Current Plan, Execution, Remaining, percent complete) are the sums of the active Budget Lines of the group in that Budget, so they equal the sum of the rows shown under it. Archived lines stay in the archive section. Groups, Categories without a group and the archive section together equal „Razem” to the grosz, also with „Pokaż zarchiwizowane” on (where „Razem” already includes archived lines, as before this change). The row shows an „over plan” signal when any active expense Category of the group exceeds its plan, even when the group as a whole has a positive Remaining. A group without an active line in the Budget has no row.

The operation selector shows groups as labels above their Categories (no selectable group); the Operations filter and the command palette stay flat.

We rejected:

- the sum of a group including archived lines under „Pokaż zarchiwizowane” (the group row would show a sum of Categories that are not listed under it, and the sum would depend on the toggle);
- „Bez grupy” as one block at the end or the start of the list (a Category without a group could not stand between groups, and the order would no longer be one sequence);
- dragging that changes the order but not the group (a Category could not be moved into a group by hand, and the two views would disagree about what a drop means);
- a change of the type of a group (the Categories in it would have to be taken off or moved with it);
- a composite foreign key that makes the database check that a Category and its group have the same type (the schema has no composite keys or CHECK constraints; the Actions and a PostgreSQL test keep the types consistent).
